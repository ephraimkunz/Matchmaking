Finding 1: No guaranteed minimum — a stable 4-6 people get chosen by nobody

The round-robin assignment enforces a maximum appearance cap (--max-appearances) but has no minimum. On your real data, this consistently leaves 4-6 of 110 people appearing on zero other people's shortlists, stable across seeds — even though those same people have full 5-person shortlists themselves. At your event's 3-course format, these are the people nobody chooses to sit with for any course.

Counterintuitively, raising the appearance cap makes it worse, not better (I tested -a 8 through -a 40): more headroom just lets already-popular people absorb more picks. I verified a fix — giving run_round/next_available a per-round preference for candidates below an appearance floor before falling back to normal ranked order — eliminates the zero-appearance group entirely in simulation, at a cost of only ~0.24% of total displayed compatibility (0.7640 → 0.7622 mean served score). That's a strong trade and I'd recommend implementing it if you want, but I didn't touch the code since you asked me to investigate, not fix.

Finding 2: The religion dealbreaker is your data's biggest bottleneck

Of 3,024 possible male-female pairs, 1,316 (44%) are eliminated by dealbreakers, and 1,122 of those (85%) are religion alone — not a code bug, this is how the filter is designed to work, but the size of the effect is worth knowing before the event. It's concentrated in a handful of people: e.g. one person eliminates 98% of the opposite sex, four others eliminate 83-93%. Most of this comes from the 29 people who chose "the same as mine" (exact match required) rather than "within one level" — the same as mine and rel 5 mostly are big commitment level 5, so it's less severe there, but low-commitment "same as mine" answers or people combined with a small commitment-level minority (levels 1-3, only 18 people total) are the worst-hit. Not something to fix in code — just useful context for the host: this is likely why some people end up with short-viable-candidate pools independent of anything else in the pipeline.
